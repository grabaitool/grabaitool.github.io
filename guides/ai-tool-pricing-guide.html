How Much Do AI Tools Cost in 2026? Real Entry Prices by Category

The honest answer: it depends on the meter

AI tools are priced four different ways — flat subscriptions, per-seat plans, credit systems and per-token API rates — which is why "how much does it cost?" rarely has a one-line answer. The figures below are the entry prices our editors verify for every listing in this directory, organised by the jobs people actually search for. Every number links to a full review with the complete pricing picture.

AI chatbots and assistants

The big assistants have settled around a standard twenty: ChatGPT Plus is ~$20/mo (with a Go tier from ~$8/mo), Claude Pro is from ~$20/mo and Perplexity Pro is from ~$20/mo — all three keep genuinely usable free plans. Google Gemini undercuts the pattern with AI Plus from ~$4.99/mo. Private and local options sit outside the subscription model entirely: Underdog runs on your own computer and is free during its invite-only beta.

AI writing and content

Writing tools spread widest. Grammarly Pro is from ~$30/mo, Copy.ai's Chat plan from ~$29/mo and Jasper Pro from ~$69/mo (~$59/mo billed annually). Writesonic now bundles its article writer with AI search visibility tracking, and its plans start around ~$99/mo — a useful reminder to check what a platform has become before comparing its price with pure writing tools.

AI video

Video is credit country. Kling 4.0 plans start at $8.80/mo for 660 credits, and the meter runs per second: 10 credits per second at 720p, 15 at 1080p. Runway paid plans start from ~$15/mo after free credits, and Descript — editing rather than generation — starts from ~$24/mo. Always convert credits into finished seconds before comparing two video tools; the cheaper plan is often the one with the slower meter, not the lower headline price.

Voice, image and design

ElevenLabs voice plans start from ~$6/mo. Midjourney image plans start from ~$10/mo, and Canva Pro is $18/mo billed monthly ($144/yr) for the design suite most small teams already know.

Code and API models

Developer pricing splits in two. IDE assistants are subscriptions — Cursor starts from ~$8/mo. Raw models are per-token: MiniMax M3.1 Flash charges $0.15 per 1M input tokens and $0.60 per 1M output, GPT-6.1 Terra charges $1 per 1M input and $5 per 1M output, and the OpenAI Decisions API charges $0.10 per 1M input with output free. At these rates the model itself is rarely the bill — the orchestration around it is.

Productivity and business

Per-seat pricing rules here, so multiply before you fall in love. Reclaim AI keeps a free Lite plan and paid tiers from ~$12/user/mo for calendar defence. Asana AI is not sold separately at all — it rides inside Asana plans from $10.99/user/mo billed annually, with AI request caps that matter more than the seat price. For design-to-engineering work, zCAD keeps a free AI tier (200 credits a month) before AI Plus at $49.99/mo.

Marketing and brand visibility

The newest category is also the most split. AI-answer visibility starts free: Dageno's Brand Explorer and Operato AI's readiness score both cost nothing. Classic monitoring starts at Awario's $29/mo and Brand24's $79/mo (both billed annually), and full SEO suites with AI layers start at Ahrefs $129/mo and Semrush $139.95/mo. Our Dageno alternatives guide compares this cluster properly.

Three pricing traps to watch

  • Credits that hide the meter. Convert every credit plan into cost per finished output — per second of video, per image, per article — before comparing.
  • Per-seat creep. A $11 tool is a $550 line item at fifty seats. Check whether AI features are capped per account, not per user.
  • Annual-billing headlines. Most "from" prices assume annual billing; monthly billing commonly runs 15–30% higher. Reclaim's published annual discount, for example, is 29%.

A realistic starter-stack budget

Abstract ranges are hard to plan with, so here is the arithmetic for a typical one-person business in 2026, using entry prices from the categories above. One AI assistant subscription for writing, research and spreadsheets: around $20 a month. One design or image tool: $10–20. One video tool, if video matters to the business: $15–30. One productivity or meeting tool: $15–20. Total: roughly $60–90 a month — less than one hour of most professionals' billed time, which is the honest comparison. Notice what is not in the stack: a second tool in any category. Duplication is where budgets quietly double, because two overlapping subscriptions each feel too cheap to cancel. Review the stack every quarter with one question per line — "what did this produce in the last 90 days?" — and cut anything without an answer.

Annual or monthly? The real maths

Annual billing typically saves 15–25%, and vendors push it hard for a reason: it converts your experiment into their retention. The maths that matters is not the discount, it is your survival rate. If there is any real chance you will drop the tool within three months — you are still testing the category, the team has not adopted it, or a competitor is improving fast — monthly billing is cheaper even at the higher rate, because three monthly payments beat twelve annual ones you never use. Rule of thumb: pay monthly until a tool has survived two renewal decisions, then switch to annual and bank the discount. And whatever you choose, put the renewal date in your calendar the day you subscribe; "set and forget" is a pricing strategy too, and it is not yours.
