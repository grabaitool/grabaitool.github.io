Best AI Headshot Generators (2026): Aragon vs HeadshotPro vs Dreamwave

AI headshot generators turn a handful of selfies into studio-style portraits for LinkedIn, resumes and company pages — for the price of a team lunch instead of a photographer's day rate. The category has consolidated around a few serious tools, and they differ where it matters: photos per dollar, turnaround time, resolution, and what happens if the batch disappoints you. We compared the three leaders in our directory.

1. Aragon AI — fastest, and the most photos to start

Aragon AI returns up to 100 headshots in about 15 minutes on its top package — the fastest turnaround here. Packages are one-time: Basic $35 for 40 headshots, Standard $45 for 60, Executive $75 for 100 with enhanced resolution and every style option. If the results miss, a free redo or a refund within 14 days applies — provided you have not downloaded anything yet, so review before you save.

2. HeadshotPro — built for teams, cheapest entry

HeadshotPro is the business-first option: companies buy credits (one credit = one person's full shoot), invite staff from a dashboard, and get consistent portraits back — with SSO and a SOC 2 Type II report for IT departments, and credits that never expire. Individuals pay $29 for 30 headshots, $39 for 50 with a free redo, or $59 for 70 with 4K downloads. Team pricing falls to roughly $19.50 per person at volume, the best per-head rate here.

3. Dreamwave — premium finish, strict policy

Dreamwave positions at the premium end: its top package adds unlimited human touch-ups, so a person finishes your portraits after the AI. Starter is $35 for 40 photos, Standard $45 for 60 with one free redo, Premium $99 for 100 with two redos and the touch-ups. It reports 25M+ portraits delivered and stores data only in the US. Read the policy first, though: Dreamwave does not refund once your photos are uploaded and processing has begun — the strictest terms in this comparison.

What to check before you pay anyone

Resolution: a LinkedIn avatar needs little; a printed conference banner needs the 4K tier. Refunds: every tool here limits them once downloads or processing start — judge the preview gallery before committing further. Privacy: you are uploading your face; check the deletion policy and whether your photos train models — Dreamwave and HeadshotPro both publish clear stances. Input quality: varied, well-lit selfies (different angles, no sunglasses) matter more than which generator you choose.

Is there a free option?

Honestly: no dedicated AI headshot generator with a genuinely free tier is worth recommending today — the leaders are all pay-first, which is exactly why the refund and redo terms above matter. The zero-cost DIY route is a good phone portrait plus a background cleanup with Remove.bg — respectable for a profile photo, but it will not give you the studio-outfit variety the paid tools generate. If the headshot is for a job application, the $29–35 entry packages are the honest price of doing it properly.

What makes a headshot look AI-made — and how to avoid it

Reviewers of these tools keep circling the same tells. Skin smoothed into plastic, with every pore and line removed, reads as artificial instantly — the better generators now keep texture, and you should pick the output that looks least retouched, not most glamorous. Teeth and ears warp at the edges of the set, so check the full-size file, not the thumbnail, before you pay for downloads. Backgrounds give the game away too: smeared office blur and impossible architecture behind an otherwise sharp face. Plain, darker or outdoor backgrounds hide this best. Finally, consistency with your other photos matters more than perfection — a headshot that looks like a different person from your video calls and event photos creates exactly the doubt a professional profile exists to remove.

The input photo checklist that decides your results

Output quality is set before the AI ever runs, by the selfies you upload. Use recent photos — within the last year, with your current hairstyle and glasses. Vary the angles and lighting across the set rather than uploading ten near-identical shots from one afternoon; the model needs to learn your face, not one expression. Keep other people, sunglasses and heavy filters out entirely. Daylight near a window beats flash, and a plain background beats a busy room. If a service reports that some uploads were rejected, replace them rather than proceeding with a thin set: generators trained on eight good photos reliably beat the same service trained on fifteen mixed ones. Ten honest minutes here saves the cost of a wasted generation round.

For teams: the consistency rules

The team plans in the tools above solve a real problem — a company page where twelve headshots have twelve different backgrounds, crops and colour temperatures looks unplanned even when every individual photo is good. Fix the variables before anyone generates: one background style, one crop ratio, one dress-code note, and one aspect — square, for directories and profile grids. Generate everyone in the same week on the same service so the underlying model version matches, and nominate one person to reject the warped or over-smoothed outliers rather than letting each employee pick their favourite. Budget holders should note the maths: per-person team pricing through a service like HeadshotPro usually undercuts a single photographer's session once you pass eight to ten staff, and new joiners can be matched to the set later without rebooking a studio day.
